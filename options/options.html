LiChessable

The Lichess opening explorer requires authentication. Create a free personal access token — leave all scope checkboxes unchecked, the explorer only needs a valid token, no permissions — and paste it below.

ChessDB (chessdb.cn) is a cloud analysis book: for opening positions it answers deeper than a local search, in about 400 ms, with no engine running at all. Auto runs Stockfish as before and shows the book's own numbers beside its lines wherever the position is genuinely in the book — the engine stays the section's main figures, the book is a second opinion. ChessDB only never starts the engine at all, and accepts no answer where the book has none — off the beaten track, and for most tactics courses' custom starting positions, that is often. Choose Stockfish only on a slow or filtered network, or to keep every position on this machine.

Auto runs the lite Stockfish build on low-power devices (phones, ≤4 cores or ≤4 GB memory) and the full build elsewhere; Full/Lite forces one. Each build must be downloaded to engine/vendor/ first — see the README's "Local engine" section.

Phones and tablets get larger type and roomier tap targets in every panel and widget this extension adds. Auto works it out from the pointer: the first finger on the screen (or a device with no mouse at all) is remembered for this browser and never forgotten, so driving the phone from a PC — scrcpy, a USB or Bluetooth mouse, DeX — keeps the mobile sizing instead of shrinking back to desktop the moment the mouse moves. Pick Touch for a phone this browser has genuinely never been touched on, and Desktop for a touchscreen laptop that was tapped once and stayed large.

In a trainer session, whenever the comment on a move you have just played offers the opponent an alternative as a clickable line, a small panel shows that branch on a mini board with an arrow — plus a button that queues the line for review as soon as the variation ends. Only the branching move is named; the rest of the line is what the review will ask you for. It disappears again once you play your next move, and it carries its own Turn off so you need not come here. The Review clickable(s) button at the end of a variation is unaffected either way.

Pinned mistakes on more than one device

A device that has never been set up here adopts a course called LiChessable by itself, if you have one. Make that course once and every other device picks it up on its own; choosing anything here — including Off — is a decision, and is never overridden.

Pins already follow Chrome Sync wherever you are signed in to Chrome with extension sync on. Most Android browsers that can run extensions have no Chrome Sync behind them, so this is the transport for those: the pins are written into a single informational card — a card with no move, which Chessable never quizzes or schedules — in a course of your own. Every device signed in to your Chessable account then reads and writes that one card. Pick the same course on each device. The card is called CLO-SYNC · do not delete; you may move it to any chapter you like, but if you delete it the pins it was carrying go with it. Nothing else about your courses is read or written, and nothing leaves your Chessable account.

A small panel in the bottom-right corner naming the move you just failed, with one button that pins it. A pinned move outlives the session: it comes back in the mistake drill after half an hour, then four hours, then a day, and retires itself once you have answered it clean three times on three separate occasions — a miss puts it back to the start. Nothing is rescheduled on Chessable and its board is never touched. The offer appears once per move, goes away by itself if you ignore it, and you can always pin from the Mistakes list instead. Pins that have come round again are offered by a launcher in that same corner, on any Chessable page.

Normally the clickable lines in a completed variation's comment wait behind the Review clickable(s) button. With this on, the review starts by itself as soon as the variation is finished and plays every line the variation has — no button, no “All / Final” question. It stays out of the way of a Retry pass, and exiting a review does not reopen it: the next variation gets the next one.

Puts a small Move… field on the in-session widget while a quiz is waiting for you, and plays what you type on Chessable's own board. Algebraic notation (Nc3, exd5, O-O, Rae1) or the two squares (e2e4); a move number, spaces and +/! are ignored. The move goes as soon as what you have typed can only mean one legal move, so there is normally nothing to press — the one exception is O-O when O-O-O is also legal, where Enter settles it (or type e1g1, which cannot mean anything else). Esc gives the keyboard back to Chessable's own shortcuts until the next move. Promotions still have to be played on the board.

Once a variation is finished the field stays where it is, empty, and Enter goes to the next variation — so a whole session can be played without letting go of the keyboard, and on a phone the on-screen keyboard does not close between variations. The next card takes the keyboard back by itself. While the field is being typed into, the page's zoom is pinned, so a phone cannot scale the board off the screen around it.

Adds a Show & grade button to the in-session widget in trainer sessions. In that mode you never make a move — a card is two presses, and every press shows a move: 2 plays the correct move on our own board, then 2 again shows the opponent's reply and says you knew it, or 4 shows the reply and marks the card wrong through Chessable's own I don't know control. At the end of a variation 2 asks for the next one, so a whole session runs from two keys.

It exists so a session can be studied from a Bluetooth ring with the screen out of reach, and it is the groundwork for blindfold practice. Your thinking time is not charged to the card: Chessable's timer is paused while you think and started again only to play the move. Promotions still have to be played on the board.

It works the same way in a Retry pass, and the same two keys answer a card in Drill mistakes — there they are simply an extra way to answer, since that board still takes a played move. An Auto toggle beside the Stop button replays the cards you have already answered correctly this session and hands back at the first one you have not; it is separate from the ordinary auto-play setting, and it never plays a move a Retry pass is walking you back to.

Off by default because the field takes the keyboard while it is focused, so MoveTrainer's single-key shortcuts (n for the next variation and the rest) go to the move field instead for as long as it has focus. Typed moves count exactly like moves played with the mouse — same scheduling, same mistake log, and they are remembered by auto-play in the same way.

Review auto-play (the toggle on the in-session Overstudy widget): auto-played moves count as normal correct answers — the move was proven earlier this session, and Chessable schedules it as passed. Failed or timed-out moves are never auto-played, and a move is never auto-played the first time it appears in a session. Nothing is remembered across browser sessions.

Difficult moves only

Switched on and off in the auto-play settings (the ⚙ on the in-session widget). It gives auto-play a second reason to play a move: not just “you answered this correctly earlier today”, but “Chessable's own lifetime record says you are reliably good at this one” — so a review session stops only on the moves you actually find hard. The two numbers below are what “reliably good” means.

A move you have never missed is always played. One you have missed is played only if it is above the accuracy bar and the last miss is older than the window — so a bad session yesterday puts a move back in your hands whatever its lifetime figure says. Set the window to 0 to go by accuracy alone.

Running out of time does not count as a mistake here, even though Chessable's own Difficult Moves page counts it: most recorded failures are timeouts rather than wrong moves, and that measures the clock rather than what you know. Answering I don't know in show-and-grade does count — it is a statement about the move.

Review sessions only (/practice/). In a learn session a move you have never missed may simply be one you have never been shown, and auto-playing brand-new material would skip the teaching; the switch has no effect there. A move it decides is hard is never auto-played even if you did answer it correctly earlier the same session. The figures are read once per course per session from the same data as Chessable's own Difficult Moves page; if they cannot be read, nothing extra is played.

The token is stored only in this browser's extension storage (chrome.storage.local) and is sent only to explorer.lichess.org. You can revoke it any time on your Lichess token page.
